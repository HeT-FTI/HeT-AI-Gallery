1. What this library does not do

Every row below is a limit that will cost somebody a day if they meet it in the field instead of here. The point of the page is not humility: a control library whose boundaries are unstated gets used outside them, and the failure then looks like a bug in the library rather than a fact about the domain.

This page is about what the library cannot do. It is a different list from honest limits, which is about what software verification cannot reach and is deferred to hardware -- dead-time, ADC timing, EMI, ISR worst case. Both are needed: one is a property of the method, the other of the evidence.

Each row names the case that pins it, and the boundaries gate checks that every named case still exists. A statement whose case was renamed or deleted therefore fails the build rather than quietly becoming folklore. That is the only reason this page can be trusted after the person who wrote it has moved on.

What it cannot do

What that means for you

Pinned by

Resolve the magnet polarity.

The injection observer converges to dtheta = pi as happily as to zero: the d axis is right and the magnet is backwards, so the torque sign is inverted. Telling them apart needs a separate pulse or saturation test, which is MagnetPolarityTest applied once at commissioning -- not something the observer does for you.

SilHfi.OutsideTheHalfTurnWindowItLocksToTheMirror

Survive a wrong saliency sign.

A machine with Ld > Lq needs demod_gain_a positive; the wrong sign does not give a worse estimate, it drives the estimate to the unstable equilibrium at +-pi/2 and leaves it there.

SilHfi.AWrongSaliencyDirectionRunsAwayInsteadOfLocking

Say "I do not know" through `ObsState`.

The angle has no validity channel. An observer that is not measuring holds its last angle and reports zero speed -- indistinguishable from a stopped machine by those two numbers alone. Use locked() (or status()["observer_locked"] from Python) to tell them apart.

SwitchableObserver.TheSelectedObserversLockVerdictIsReachableThroughTheSlot

Run an injection carrier the control rate cannot resolve.

Past hfi_max_inject_hz(control_hz) the observer stops: the demodulated amplitude collapses, the lock drops out, the angle freezes, the speed reads zero, and a loop closing on it runs away (measured: -19.5 rad/s of speed bias). The request is refused at setup, and by update() for the paths that do not go through the bridge.

HfiObserver.ACarrierThisControlRateCannotResolveIsRefusedNotFrozen

Use one carrier frequency at every control rate.

The ceiling is a sampling fact, not a machine fact: 9200 Hz is refused at 20 kHz and accepted at 40 kHz. Sizing a carrier for one rate and reusing it at another is the mistake this row exists to prevent.

HfiObserver.TheCarrierCeilingScalesWithTheControlRate

Run the injection observer on a surface machine.

predicted_gain_a() is proportional to (lq - ld), so with lq == ld the demodulated signal is identically zero and no follower can lock. The mode is refused rather than admitted and left to fail later.

ApiObserverSelector.TheHfiModeRefusesWithoutACarrierOrWithoutSaliency

Acquire a rotor that is already turning.

The loop pulls the angle only inside +-pi/2 and has no way to pull a frequency it has never seen. A rotor handed to it mid-spin either gets caught or lands on the mirror, and which one is a property of the speed. A hand-over must seed it (seed_angle), which is what the blending observer does while the carrier is wound down.

SilHfiEnvelope.AcquiringARotorAlreadyRunningNeedsASeed

Reduce the low-speed servo's residual ripple by improving the angle.

The steady bias at 20 rad/s is 0.0039 rad/s (0.02 %); what remains is a ripple at the carrier frequency, and it is the injected current cross-coupling into torque on a turning machine -- linear in inject_v and in speed, indifferent to the saliency. Improving the angle by 13x does not move it. inject_v is the knob.

SilSpeedRipple.TheFigureSurvivesSubstepResolution

Reconstruct what the hardware actually did from a trace.

The trace records what the loop believed: the currents the PI acted on and the voltages it applied before modulation. A trace that looks perfect is therefore entirely consistent with a miscalibrated current sensor -- the loop is faithful to a wrong measurement. Use it to explain the loop's decisions, not to measure the phases.

Trace.TheRecordAgreesWithTheScalarReadBitForBit

Start a load the drive cannot move.

The sequence ramps to its limit, holds, and after its grace period reports Failed with zero torque: measured 0.200 N*m at 0.5 N*m/s reached at 400 ms, then a 200 ms grace. It cannot conjure torque -- the only honest answers are "released" and "cannot start", and from outside a silent axis is indistinguishable from a working one, which is why the failure is a state rather than an absence.

StallStart.AStuckAxisSaysFailedInsteadOfRampingForever

See a fault code while the drive is derating.

A hot machine below the trip temperature reports fault = None and active_mask = 0 while its current limit is already falling -- measured: 3.00 A at 45 C, 1.80 A at 80 C, both with output allowed, then 0 A and no output at 105 C. output_allowed is defined as active_mask == 0, so a fault means "authority removed" and derating is not one: read i_limit_a against the i_max_a you configured if you want to know the machine is hot. A commissioning tool that shows only the fault code will report a derating drive as healthy.

Protection.TheTemperatureLadderIsGradedRatherThanACliff

Estimate a torque that accounts for iron loss or magnet temperature.

torque_estimate() is kt*iq + 1.5*pp*(ld-lq)*id*iq, and kt is a nameplate number: it does not know that the magnet weakens as it heats, that iron loss subtracts torque at speed, or that the inverter adds no torque. The 5 % agreement it reports is against the same model the plant integrates, so it is an agreement about the model, not a claim about a hot machine.

TorqueMap.TheEstimateMatchesTheMechanicsOfTheSilBench

Improve a sensored axis's angle by adding the carrier.

The sensor owns the commutation angle, so the injection observer now runs for its own demodulation only: the carrier does reach the inverter (measured swing 0.45--0.67 across eight rate/PWM combinations), but the sensor keeps the angle and the published speed. status()["observer"] naming HFI therefore does not mean the loop is commutating on the injected estimate -- compare the two explicitly if that is what you need.

MotorInjection.ASensoredAxisInjectsWithoutSurrenderingTheAngle

Get a quiet speed estimate out of the injection observer for free.

omega_e = omega_int + kp*error, and error carries the carrier, so the published speed holds 0.4180 rad/s at 2000 Hz in a 20 rad/s servo unless speed_lpf_hz is set. The library's own path sets it (200 Hz); a caller that constructs the observer directly gets the raw value.

SilSpeedRipple.ThePublishedSpeedCanBeCleanedAndTheRotorDoesNotCare

Close a servo on a ten-millisecond-old speed.

Differencing the angle is quieter than the PLL and worse in a loop: at a 200 rad/s speed-loop bandwidth the window is transport delay in the feedback path, and no window length from 500 us to 10 ms wins on both settled accuracy and peak. The route is closed by measurement, not by taste.

SilSpeedFromAngleClosedLoop.AWindowShortEnoughToBeOnTimeIsTheOnlyWayThisRouteWins

Get a rotor angle out of three Hall sensors alone.

The code carries 60 degrees of information per edge and nothing inside a sector: two angles 59.96 degrees apart read the same code, so a decoder that sees only the code is wrong by up to 30 degrees -- worst case, half a sector, and no cleverness improves it. Ten degrees of mounting error on one sensor widens the bound to 35 and narrows the other sectors to 25 without ever producing an illegal code, so misplacement is invisible to the wiring. Everything finer than 60 degrees has to come from edge timing, which is the decoder's work, not the sensor's.

HallPhysics.InsideOneSectorTheCodeCarriesNoAngleInformation

See a dead Hall sensor all the time.

A stuck sensor freezes its bit: one sixth of a revolution reads as the illegal code 0 or 7 (that is the entire visible fault) and the other five sixths read as perfectly legal codes at 120-degree resolution, with the edge interval doubled. Worse, every surviving transition still flips exactly one bit, so the adjacency check that most decoders carry never fires -- measured, not assumed, and the first version of that case asserted the opposite. Anything built on Halls must watch the illegal window and the edge timing; either alone has a blind spot.

HallPhysics.ADeadSensorIsSilentForFiveSixthsOfTheRevolutionAndKeepsEveryRemainingCodeLegal

Declare a dead Hall sensor over the whole revolution.

Both channels together still leave 8.4 % of a revolution reading `Ok` while wrong by up to 59 degrees (13.7 % and 120 degrees with the sensor stuck high): the illegal window is 15.5 % and the interval check another 53.9 %, so roughly a third of every revolution is undeclared. A drive built on Halls needs a second opinion -- current or torque sanity, an index, anything independent of the codes -- rather than a cleverer check on the codes. This is the number that decides how the fault has to be handled, and it is asserted so it cannot quietly become "the fault is caught".

HallTracker.AStuckSensorIsCoveredByTwoChannelsAndNeitherAloneIsEnough

Cancel friction you have not measured.

The compensation is a torque of its own, so an estimate above the machine's breakaway moves the rotor even when the reference is held still -- measured, 5.625 mrad of travel with no command at all from a (0.030, 0.030) estimate on a machine whose breakaway is 0.020. Below the breakaway it is safe but it takes the residual with it: (static - coulomb)/kp follows the estimate wherever it goes, 2.021 mrad at the true value and 1.007 at a 50 % over-estimate. There is nothing in the code stream that can tell you the estimate is wrong -- it has to come from a measurement, and the boundary of "good enough" is the breakaway.

FrictionComp.AnEstimateAboveTheBreakawayWalksTheAxisWithNoCommandAtAll

Tell a stalled axis from a friction estimate that is too low.

Both read as "at rest while the loop asks for more than the estimate", which is exactly what stalled reports, so the flag is honest about being one fact and not a diagnosis. Distinguishing them needs a second signal -- a torque measurement, an encoder that is not moving, or a command that would obviously move a healthy axis.

FrictionComp.TheStallFlagNeedsBothStillnessAndADemandAboveTheBreakaway

Stop an axis at a wall without knowing the drive's delay.

The velocity ceiling is v^2 <= 2*a*(d - v*dt) and dt is the drive's own latency, not the control period: on the SIL axis it is about 1 ms (one period plus the 0.8 ms current rise). Assuming it is zero -- the natural reading of "it can decelerate at a" -- crosses the wall by 19 mm at 20 rad/s, which is exactly v*dt; with it the axis stops 1.04 mm short. It cannot be derived from anything in the library, so it has to come from a commissioning run or a datasheet, and the number passed in should be the conservative one.

SoftLimit.TheDelayTermIsTheDifferenceBetweenNineteenMillimetresPastAndOneShort

Synchronise two axes with a position-only gear.

A perfectly geared pair still shows a standing error of v*kd/kp -- 25.6 mrad at 10 rad/s with kd 0.02 and kp 8 -- because a position loop following a moving reference always lags it. It scales with the rate and inversely with the gain, so raising kp buys accuracy at the price of stability, and the only term that removes it is the gear's velocity output fed forward as +kd*v_ref. A gear that emits only a position is a lag generator with a ratio.

ElectronicGear.TheSyncErrorIsTheRampLagInBothOfItsTerms

Trust `v_max / psi_f` for the top speed.

It over-promises by about 4 %: the d-axis drop we*Lq*iq is part of the bus too. At 24 V and this nameplate the naive ceiling is 2910 rad/s electrical and the machine's own steady state reaches 2794, and weakening by id = -2 A lifts it to 3320 -- a factor of 1.188, not the 1.202 the flux ratio promises, because the weakening current's own d-axis voltage eats part of the margin it buys. Both numbers come from bisecting the voltage that holds the operating point, not from a datasheet.

HighSpeed.TheVoltageCeilingIsFourPercentBelowTheNaiveRatioAndWeakeningLiftsIt

Read the open-loop flux observer's angle at speed without knowing its bias.

Seeded and fed correctly it still carries a 0.12 rad mean error at 200 rad/s electrical and 0.08 at 600 -- a bias, not a sampling effect, which is why it is not speed-proportional and why the number has to be measured for the machine in front of you rather than assumed small. What is speed-independent at a fixed samples-per-cycle ratio and degrades 4.7x when the ratio drops from 40 to 8 is the sampling error on top of it.

HighSpeed.TheErrorFollowsSamplesPerCycleRatherThanTheSpeed

Compute in double when the caller passes a double.

The _f64 entry points exist so a call site does not lose a value to an implicit narrowing; the library still computes in float32 throughout, and there is no wider range behind them. 1e40 is the value that says so: it is a finite double and an inf in float, and it is refused rather than accepted -- the check that decides is on the narrowed value, where it enters the loop, because judging the argument that arrived would let an infinity through. A caller sizing anything against a double range is sizing against a range the library does not have.

ApiC.AFiniteDoubleThatOverflowsFloatIsRefusedInsteadOfReachingTheLoopAsInfinity

1.1. Deferred to your hardware, not to us

Four conclusions cannot be reached from this repository at all -- not "not yet", but "not by this tree", because no board has executed it here (what has actually been run). They are listed here so that meeting them in the field reads as a known limit rather than as a defect, and docs/sphinx/source/guides/on-machine-acceptance.rst is the procedure that reaches the rest:

Why

What it means for you

Any conclusion above roughly 800 rad/s electrical

The small motors this was designed around cannot get there

The high-speed statements stay at their present level until a machine that reaches it is measured

Intermittent failures, such as a start-up hand-over that fails one time in n

They need hours to days of reproduction, which a person who has not seen the failure cannot arrange

A field report of one is a lead, not yet a diagnosis

Thermal, EMC and long-term ageing behaviour

No chamber, no instruments, no years

The absolute parameters of one particular industrial motor

The bench gives the shape of saturation, which transfers between machines; the numbers do not

Use the shape to choose a model; measure your own machine to fill it in

1.2. Claims this project does not make

The rows above are limits of the method: things the library cannot do. The rows below are a different kind of statement -- capabilities somebody reasonably expects from a field-oriented control library, and that this project deliberately does not claim. They are listed so that meeting one in the field reads as a decision rather than as an omission.

They carry no "pinned by" column, and that is not an oversight. A limit is pinned by the case that measures it; a claim that is not made is pinned by the absence of that measurement, which is what the level in the claim row already says. The claim table in the project README carries the number and the case; this page carries the sentence that may not be said.

Not claimed

Why

"Efficient", or any efficiency figure.

Nothing here measures input power against shaft power. A modulator and a control loop set the shape of the voltages; efficiency lives in the switching devices, the winding and the modulation index, none of which this project models or measures.

Thermal protection in the sense of knowing the winding temperature.

protection integrates a copper-loss budget with one time constant. That is not a temperature, and the derating thresholds are numbers the caller supplies. Without a thermistor or a thermal observer it cannot tell you how hot the motor is -- and it says so instead of reporting a plausible degree count.

"Works down to a few milliamps."

The current-loop evidence starts at 0.1 A. Below that the resolution of the model is the limit rather than the ADC, and a model says nothing about a real sensor's noise, offset drift or amplifier settling.

A sensorless low-speed servo as good as a sensored one.

Measured, and the answer is no. The gap is a property of the speed estimate rather than of the loop, and improving the angle does not close it -- the claim row carries both numbers, and the direction is what matters here.

A sensorless procedure that names its own minimum speed.

Measured to be impossible from this evidence: the settled-window error is not monotone in speed, so "the smallest speed at which an angle may be claimed" is not an interval and no procedure can hand one out. The closed form the claim row derives is a lower bound.

MTPA paired with a single-inductance observer.

MTPA is defined by a d-axis current, and those observers carry a constant flux target, so the angle error grows with d current at low speed. The pairing is refused by configuration rather than left to the reader: DualInductanceObserver is the member that makes MTPA reachable, and the refusal applies to the single-L observers, not to MTPA itself.

"The speed loop is validated past base speed."

Not established. Field weakening engages end to end through Motor, but at that operating point the q-axis current settles with a sign the PI's own contract says cannot happen. That is an open measurement rather than a resolved one, and it is recorded as open.

Anything about a specific motor.

Every number here is a property of the simulation plant and a stated nameplate. BoardSelfTest and check_board() exist to tell you how far your hardware is from that model -- and they are themselves unverified on metal, which is what the level in their claim row says.

1.3. Not a safety component

This library is not certified, not redundant and not fail-safe. protection is a supervisory layer that assumes the MCU, the gate driver and the sensing are already working: it cannot protect against a failed current sensor, a shorted bridge or a lost PWM. Do not use it in any application where a wrong torque command can injure somebody.

The latching behaviour exists to make a fault inspectable -- a fault that stays until somebody clears it is one a person can read after the fact -- not to make the drive safe. A latch is a diagnostic, and a diagnostic is not a safeguard. Where the supervisory layer does have teeth is What may be claimed, and to whom, which states the sentences this project may and may not say about it.

What is not on this page, because it is enforced by a gate rather than by a test case: no exceptions, no RTTI, no new/delete, no std::vector/std::string, C++17 and C11 only (strict), every installed header carrying a README claim row (claims), and every number in the documentation matching an artefact the build produced (docs).

This page says what the library does not do. What it may say — the sentences that are allowed, the tiers behind them, and the answer each industry gets — is What may be claimed, and to whom; the equivalent for a single capability is What each capability offers, and what it costs.
